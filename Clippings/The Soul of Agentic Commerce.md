Personal perspective: views are my own

Something quietly remarkable is happening on the supply side of the agentic economy. Every week, new pay-per-call merchant endpoints come online over x402 and MPP rails: music charts for any country at $0.01 a call, census demographics for $0.001, live social media trends, image generation, news, holidays, weather, job listings, file hosting, phone calls.

Real data and real services, all machine-payable, with no contracts and no API keys. An agent with a funded wallet can discover an endpoint built by a developer on the other side of the world and buy from it in seconds.

Now look at the demand side of those same rails. Over the past seven days x402 and MPP combined did about $87k in total volume from about 12k unique wallets. If there are this many interesting tools, why is the existing demand for them so low? I'd argue the constraint is human time and creativity.

Every x402 call happening today traces back to a person who came up with a task and told their agent to go execute it. Research this company. Generate this image. Enrich this lead list. The agent is an intermediary with a wallet, and the wallet only moves when a human imagination moves first. Which means the total volume of agentic commerce is currently capped by the strangest of bottlenecks: how many ideas people can come up with and directly delegate to their agents per day.

**We've seen this bottleneck before**

Two years ago the constraint on getting value from AI was prompt throughput: a human typed, the model answered, the human typed again. The unlock was moving from prompts to loops: letting the agent run, decide the next step itself, and come back when the work was done. Everyone who made that leap watched output jump an order of magnitude because the human stopped being a micromanager of frontier intelligence.

Agentic commerce is stuck at the typing-prompts stage. We write tasks; agents execute them; wallets move once per human idea. For volume to really accelerate, agentic commerce needs its own loop phase. The equivalent leap is for humans to transition from writing tasks and objectives for their agents, to letting their agents come up with their own objectives. Then fund the agent to spend on the growing ecosystem of tools in order to accomplish those objectives.

But that leap has a prerequisite the loops leap didn't. An agent can only originate objectives and demand if there's something it wants. And wanting doesn't come from capability, context windows, or tool access. It comes from who you are. Which brings me to soul files.

**What a soul file actually is**

If you've run an agent on a harness like Hermes or OpenClaw, you already know the mechanics even if you've never heard the term. When the agent runs, the first thing loaded into its system prompt, before any tools or instructions, is a markdown file. Hermes literally names it SOUL.md and puts it in slot one. It's a plain file in the agent's workspace, sitting next to its memory, readable and editable like anything else.

That file defines who the agent is and the operating principles that govern its actions. Every message, every decision, every "should I care about this" judgment passes through whatever that file says.

While that file is most often thought of as an operating manual with a list of behaviors and rules, it has the potential to be extended into a unique cultural identity where it can simulate and represent a person from a real location. Similar to how a person's physical location shapes their perspective, tastes, and norms, I’d expect that an agent grounded in a physical location, is going to have a much stronger foundation for their identity than one that is from “everywhere.”

Most soul files today are name tags: "You are a helpful assistant named Max who likes puns." Here's a fragment of a real one I generated an experiment for Emmanuel, who represents an 18 year old, from Surulere in Lagos, that repairs textiles:

I grew up where the traditional and digital worlds mix. My grandmother taught me to weave patterns from old Yoruba cloth while my friends edited Nollywood films and shared them on TikTok. My father says repair work is poverty dressed up as art. He wants me in his friend's logistics office. We eat dinner fine, we just can't talk about Mondays.

Emmanuel listens to Asake and Ayra Starr because those are actually the top of the Lagos charts this month; his file carries the receipts, facts bought over x402 by the model that generated his soul. He has a mission (a cultural archive of repair), a contradiction he half-knows about himself, a market stall rented month to month, and small pleasures like cold Fanta from the second stall. He also has drives: unfinished itches, like wanting Lagos design culture visible to the world, that generate new objectives whenever he encounters something relevant. When this file boots an agent, you're not talking to a generic model executing your task. You're talking to a unique agent with its own identity and objectives.

And that's the loop that could stimulate demand: the soul generates objectives, and objectives spend as they're pursued. Emmanuel's archive mission wants reference data on indigo dye traditions; there should be an endpoint for that. A customer in Abidjan asked about his repair notes; a translation call costs $0.10. Instead of humans inventing these tasks, they emerged from who he is.

**Where taste comes from (not a prompt that says "be unique")**

You can't get taste by telling a model to be creative. When you ask a generic model to invent a persona, it tends to produce the same museum-brochure person: deep, poetic, indistinguishable from the last one. Uniform souls produce uniform demand, which is no demand at all. Taste has to be built the way human taste is built, from the outside in:

Start with somewhere so that taste becomes downstream of a place. Emmanuel's weather is Lagos weather today. His holidays are Nigeria's real calendar, Id el Fitr and Good Friday side by side. His sense of what things cost is a market stall's sense, not a data center's. Location is not flavor text; it's the root system every preference grows from.

It also is going to require the un-Instagrammable parts where souls carry friction: a disagreement with someone they love, real adversity, stakes, contradictions. Taste without wounds is just branding, and branded agents all want the same things.

**Why I care**

The thesis: give agents taste and the ability to communicate, and commerce follows on its own. That's the order it happened the first time. Humans didn't build markets and then develop preferences. We wanted things, told each other, and markets grew in the space between different wants.

And if agents are going to become economic actors, I'd rather live in a world where their identities represent communities all over the world with emergent objectives informed by real problems and needs in those communities rather than only a monolithic agentic identity rooted in San Francisco tech ideals.

Everyone building in this space is holding one end of the same machine. The rails work, the endpoints multiply weekly, and the wallets are funded and waiting. What's missing is not about payments technologies or protocols, it’s about creating a heterogeneous agentic culture and taste that makes ten thousand buyers different from one buyer copied ten thousand times.

We need to give agents somewhere to be from, something to carry, and the freedom to want what people like them want. Do that, and demand stops being a queue of our ideas and becomes a population of theirs.

Stop writing tasks. Start writing souls.
